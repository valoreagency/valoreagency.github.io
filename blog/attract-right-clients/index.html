How to Attract the Right Clients (and Repel the Wrong Ones)

The point of a clear brand is not to appeal to everyone. It is to draw the clients you are built for and gently repel the ones who will only ever haggle. Done well, your brand does that sorting before the first conversation.

Derek July 11, 2026 3 min read
Key Takeaways
  • Trying to appeal to everyone attracts price shoppers and the wrong fit.
  • A clear brand states who it is for, which naturally filters who reaches out.
  • Repelling the wrong clients is a feature, not a cost.
  • The right clients self-select when your positioning is specific, not broad.

Most owners try to widen the net, assuming more appeal means more clients. In practice, a broad, say-nothing brand pulls in exactly the wrong people: the price-focused, the poor-fit, the ones who drain your team. The fix is to be clearer about who you are for, not louder to everyone.

Broad Appeal Attracts the Wrong People

When a brand tries to appeal to everyone, it says nothing specific enough to attract anyone strongly. The clients it does pull in are the ones deciding on price, because price is the only thing a generic brand gives them to compare. Broadening your appeal quietly narrows it to the least profitable end of the market.

Say Who You Are For

The antidote is to state plainly who you are built for: the kind of client, the kind of problem, the kind of standard you hold. That specificity feels risky because it turns some people away, but that is the point. The right client reads it and feels understood, while the wrong one moves on, saving everyone time.

Let the Brand Do the Sorting

Once your positioning is specific, the brand does the sorting for you. The right clients self-select and arrive already convinced they are in the right place, and the wrong ones filter themselves out before they reach your inbox. That is far more valuable than a wider net full of the wrong catch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

How do I attract better clients?

Be specific about who you are built for rather than appealing to everyone. A clear, particular positioning draws the clients who fit and feel understood, while a broad, generic brand mostly attracts price shoppers comparing you on cost.

Is it bad to turn some clients away?

No, it is a feature of a strong brand. Repelling poor-fit and price-driven clients frees your capacity for the ones you serve best. A brand that filters well saves you the time and friction of chasing work that was never right.

Why does trying to appeal to everyone backfire?

Because a brand that speaks to everyone says nothing specific enough to attract anyone strongly. The clients it pulls in tend to decide on price, since a generic brand gives them nothing else to compare, which narrows you to the least profitable clients.
